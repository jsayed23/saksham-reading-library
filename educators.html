For educators

Saksham is a small reading library that students can configure to their own brain — font, contrast, pace, motion, and audio — before they begin. This page is the dashboard you'd see for a class using it, plus everything you need to use it yourself.

The fastest possible start

  1. Open the library on any device with a modern browser.
  2. Hand it to the student.
  3. Tell them: "Change the font and the colors until reading feels okay. Then pick a passage."

That's it. No accounts, no install, no signup, no data collection. Their settings save on the device they use.

What's actually in the library

Eight short passages — about three to four minutes each — organized into three categories: Stories, Science, and Sky & stars. Reading level is "Easy" or "Medium." All eight are appropriate for roughly 10–16 year olds. Several are set in Indian contexts (Mumbai, banyan trees, taal music, nakshatras) — chosen because the original research interviews were with Indian educators and families.

A 30-minute classroom routine

  1. Minutes 0–5. Let students set up their preferences. Tell them they can change anything later, and that the settings save automatically. Resist the urge to choose for them — this is the lesson.
  2. Minutes 5–20. Each student picks one passage and reads it. Audio is optional. They can change settings mid-passage. Your job is to be available for questions, not to monitor pace.
  3. Minutes 20–30. If you want a writing or discussion follow-up, ask one prompt per passage. For example: "What's one thing you remember from the banyan tree?" or "What did the lamp lighter teach his granddaughter without saying it?"

How to differentiate without building scaffolds

Each setting in Saksham covers a kind of differentiation you would otherwise be doing manually:

  • Students with dyslexia can switch to OpenDyslexic font and turn on read-aloud. The current word will be highlighted as it's spoken.
  • Students with ADHD can turn motion to "Reduced" or "None" to remove transitions, and switch to high-contrast theme to reduce visual noise.
  • Students with autism who shut down at bright screens can switch to warm cream or dark theme.
  • Students with low vision can use the Atkinson Hyperlegible font at Large or Extra Large size.
  • Students who are still emerging readers can read alongside audio at a slower pace (0.5–0.7×) and see each word highlight in real time.

You don't need to know which student needs which. They figure it out. That's the design.

Privacy

No data leaves the device. Saksham does not have a backend, an account system, or any analytics. All settings and progress are stored in the student's browser using localStorage. You can verify this yourself by opening the browser's network tab — nothing is uploaded.

This matters in school contexts. You can hand Saksham to a minor without parental consent forms because there is no data to consent to share.

What Saksham is not

  • It is not a curriculum. It is a calm reading surface.
  • It is not a measurement tool. There are no scores, no time-on-task, no comparison to other students. By design.
  • It is not a replacement for an accessibility specialist. It just removes one small piece of improvisation from your day.
